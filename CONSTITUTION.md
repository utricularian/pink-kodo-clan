# Constitution of the Pink Kodo Clan

**Status:** Initial draft for guild review  
**Effective date:** To be recorded upon adoption

This Constitution establishes a council government for the Pink Kodo Clan (the **Guild**). It is intended to be clear enough to guide decisions while remaining adaptable through the amendment process below. The one-time launch arrangement is recorded separately in [INITIAL_GOVERNMENT_LAUNCH.md](INITIAL_GOVERNMENT_LAUNCH.md).

## 1. Governing principles

1. The Guild is governed by its members through elected Elders, Guild-wide Edicts, and the procedures in this Constitution.
2. The Council should remain accessible to members and allow leadership to change when elected leaders wish to step down or members choose different leadership.
3. Private deliberation may be used to prepare proposals and handle misconduct cases. Votes are public except where this Constitution expressly requires anonymous or private ballots.
4. No law or portfolio decision may conflict with this Constitution. If an appendix conflicts with this Constitution, this Constitution controls.
5. The Guild’s governance should not change back and forth so quickly that members cannot understand which rules are in force. The seven-day change cooldown in Section 6.2 applies to all laws, including constitutional amendments.

## 2. Bill of Rights

The following rights are protected parts of this Constitution. They may not be changed through an ordinary Administrative Law or Edict. Changing these protected rights requires a total reformation of Guild governance: the Council and this Constitution cease to govern, and a new governance arrangement must be created from the beginning.

### 2.1 Administrative Guild Master

The in-game Guild Master rank, held by the parked level-one character **The Pink Kodo**, is an administrative role. It does not give its human operator independent governing authority under this Constitution. If that person needs to step away, the Guild Master rank must be transferred to another serving Elder so the Guild remains administratively operable.

### 2.2 Access to leadership

Every member who is not a Pink Horn may be nominated, may campaign, and may be elected to an elected Elder seat, subject to the nomination procedure in Appendix A. Incumbency creates no special advantage or entitlement to another term.

### 2.3 Freedom to step down

Any member may resign an elected position or voluntarily demote themselves at any time and for any reason. Members should be able to do so without retaliation or stigma. Members planning an absence of three weeks or longer are strongly encouraged to resign an officer role so that an active member can serve.

### 2.4 Freedom to abstain

Any member may abstain from any vote. The consequences of an abstention depend on the vote type and are stated in this Constitution. The special Impeachment Vote rule is an exception: an eligible Elder’s abstention or absence counts as assent, and an explicit “no” is required to block impeachment.

### 2.5 Vote records

Administrative votes and Elder elections are public, named votes. Edict ballots are anonymous, with the tally and result published. Impeachment and Sentencing ballots and case deliberations are private to protect the person reporting misconduct and the accused. These categories are the only standing exceptions to public vote records.

## 3. Offices, ranks, and electorate

### 3.1 Elders and the Council

The Council consists of Elder seats, initially three in total: one Anchor Elder seat and two elected Elder seats. The number of seats may be changed only by constitutional amendment through an Edict.

Elected Elders serve six-month terms. Their elections and vacancies are governed by Appendix A. The Council should, where practical, keep elected seats on different election cycles to avoid an unnecessarily busy election schedule. This is a guideline, not a requirement; election periods may overlap.

Every Elder has one vote on Elder matters and may be assigned one or more Portfolios by Administrative Law. An Elder may resign at any time. The Anchor is an Elder in every capacity and has the same ordinary Council vote and Portfolio responsibilities as any other Elder.

### 3.2 Anchor Elder

The Anchor Elder is selected by the other Elders when the Anchor seat is vacant and serves indefinitely unless the Anchor resigns, is replaced for inactivity under Section 3.4, or is removed through a successful Winds of Change vote under Section 6.4.

The Anchor’s ordinary Elder powers and duties are the same as those of other Elders. The Anchor has the additional power to resolve a tied Elder Council vote by choosing which option prevails, or to abstain. The Anchor also resolves a tie in an Elder election as described in Appendix A. The Anchor does not break ties in Edict votes.

When the Anchor seat becomes vacant, the serving Elders select a new Anchor by an Elder-only vote. The selection uses the Administrative Law approval threshold in Section 5.2. If a Guild-wide snap election is also required to fill an Elder vacancy, the resulting Council may select the Anchor from among its serving Elders; the Council must maintain the seat count authorized by this Constitution.

### 3.3 Member ranks

- **Kodo Rider:** A trusted member who may invite players to the Guild without prior authorization and may vote in Edicts.
- **Herd Member:** A member who may vote in Edicts but does not have the Kodo Rider invitation permission.
- **Pink Horn:** A new member during their first seven full days in the Guild. Pink Horns may not vote, nominate or concur on an Elder candidate, or run for an Elder seat. After seven full days, a Pink Horn automatically becomes a Herd Member.

Guild rank and the corresponding Discord role should be kept aligned. For governance purposes, the Discord account and its Guild roles determine a person’s eligibility. Votes are cast by Discord user, not by in-game character; the Guild relies on members not to evade the one-person, one-vote rule by using alternate accounts.

### 3.4 Inactivity and replacement

Logging in on any character counts as Guild activity. An Elder’s absence for 21 consecutive days does not automatically remove them. After that period, the other Elders may replace the inactive Elder through an Administrative Law vote. The affected Elder is ineligible to vote on their own replacement; the replacement motion must receive the required two-thirds of Elder seats in explicit yes votes. The resulting vacancy is filled under Appendix A. These rules apply to the Anchor as an Elder.

## 4. Portfolios

Portfolios are assigned, changed, and removed by Administrative Law. The initial Portfolios are:

- **Administrator:** Maintains governance operations, sets up and administers votes, publishes laws and decisions, and maintains the official governance record.
- **Recruiter:** Directs whether the Guild is recruiting and what kinds of members it seeks, and coordinates Pink Horn recruitment. Kodo Riders retain their rank-based ability to invite members without prior authorization, subject to any applicable laws.

These descriptions intentionally allow Elders room to define practical details. Elders are encouraged to codify recurring responsibilities through Administrative Laws. Additional Portfolio details are in Appendix C.

## 5. Laws and voting

Guild laws are either **Administrative Laws** or **Edict Laws**. An Elder authors each proposal. A non-Elder with an idea is encouraged to approach one or more Elders privately and politely to ask whether an Elder will sponsor it. This is guidance, not a formal prerequisite for raising an idea, and an Elder’s disagreement should not result in public embarrassment or retaliation.

### 5.1 Administrative Laws

Administrative Laws govern the operation and administration of the Guild without changing its broad direction. Examples include guild-bank organization and permissions, Kodo Rider promotions, Portfolio assignments, and Discord channel administration.

An Elder writes the proposed law and discusses it in the Elder-private channels. There is no minimum discussion period. Any Elder other than the author may place it before the Council for a vote. The vote remains open for seven full days. Results and each Elder’s vote are published to Guild members in a read-only record.

An Administrative Law passes only if at least two-thirds of all filled Elder seats explicitly vote yes. Fractions are rounded up to the next whole Elder. Abstentions, absences, and failures to vote are not yes votes. A vacancy is not a filled seat. If an Elder is barred from voting by this Constitution, their seat remains in the threshold unless the relevant rule says otherwise. The Anchor may resolve a genuinely tied Elder decision, but may not waive the two-thirds explicit-yes requirement.

### 5.2 Edict Laws

Edicts decide major direction or policies that materially affect members’ everyday Guild experience, such as raid schedules, roleplay requirements, or whether the Guild focuses on PvP. If it is unclear whether a proposal is administrative or an Edict, it should be treated as an Edict.

An Elder writes the proposed Edict and discusses it in Elder-private channels for at least three full days before the public ballot is opened. The ballot remains open for seven full days and is anonymous. The tally and result are published after voting closes.

The eligible electorate consists of Discord users with Elder, Kodo Rider, or Herd Member roles when the ballot opens. Pink Horns are excluded. The eligible roster is fixed at the opening of the ballot. A Discord user may cast one ballot.

An Edict passes only if both of these conditions are met:

1. At least 20% of eligible voters cast a ballot of any kind. Abstentions count toward turnout; absences do not.
2. At least two-thirds of the combined yes and no votes are yes. Abstentions are excluded from this calculation. If no yes or no votes are cast, the Edict does not pass.

The Anchor does not break ties in Edict votes.

### 5.3 General voting rules

Administrative Law and Edict Law ballots last seven full days. Elder elections, misconduct votes, Winds of Change votes, and dissolution votes follow the specific procedures in this Constitution and its appendices. The Administrator publishes the proposal, vote opening and closing times, electorate rule, outcome, and vote record required for that vote type.

## 6. Amendments, protected rights, and special votes

### 6.1 Ordinary constitutional amendments

An amendment to any part of this Constitution outside the protected Bill of Rights is an Edict and follows the Edict process and thresholds. The Bill of Rights cannot be amended by an ordinary Edict or Administrative Law; changing it requires total reformation as described in Section 6.3.

Appendices are subordinate to this Constitution. Changes to the election or misconduct procedures in Appendices A and B follow the Edict process. Portfolio responsibilities and other operational details in Appendix C may be changed by Administrative Law when they do not alter protected rights, rank-based voting eligibility, or any other constitutional requirement.

### 6.2 Seven-day change cooldown

A passed law, including a constitutional amendment passed as an Edict, cannot be amended or repealed during the seven full days after it passes. The cooldown begins immediately when the vote closes and the proposal passes.

### 6.3 Total reformation

Total reformation means the Council and this Constitution cease to govern and Guild governance is designed again from the beginning. At present, the only process established here that can dissolve the Council and Constitution is the appointment-failure process in Section 6.5. This Constitution does not create a member-initiated vote to remove the Council or dissolve the Constitution.

The absence of a member-initiated process is not an entrenched right. A future constitutional amendment may add such a process if an Elder sponsors the amendment and it passes as an Edict. No such process exists unless and until that amendment is adopted.

### 6.4 Winds of Change

An Elder may propose a Winds of Change vote to remove the Anchor. More than half of all filled Elder seats must explicitly approve opening the vote. The subsequent Guild-wide vote is an Edict, is anonymous, and lasts seven full days. It passes under the Edict turnout and approval thresholds in Section 5.2.

If it passes, the Anchor is demoted to Kodo Rider and a snap election is initiated for the resulting Elder vacancy. Once that seat is filled, the serving Elders select an Anchor under Section 3.2. Once a Winds of Change vote closes, whether it passes or fails, no further Winds of Change vote may be opened for one calendar year. The Anchor may vote in this Edict unless otherwise ineligible under this Constitution.

### 6.5 Appointment failure and dissolution

If an Elder cannot be appointed because an elected Elder election has no clear winner or the Elders cannot select an Anchor when the Anchor seat is vacant, an Elder-only dissolution vote automatically opens. An elected Elder election has no clear winner if no one runs, no one votes, or the vote is tied and the Anchor refuses or is unable to choose a winner. This appointment-failure process is the only automatic trigger for the vote.

The dissolution vote lasts seven full days and is public. A single yes vote from any Elder is sufficient to dissolve the Council and this Constitution; the first yes vote ends the ballot and dissolves the government. If no Elder votes yes by the close of the seven-day period, this Constitution deliberately prescribes no further outcome.

Upon dissolution, this Constitution and Council no longer govern, and the Guild Master resumes leadership and decision-making. Dissolution of governance does not itself disband the Guild.

## 7. Misconduct and member safety

Any Guild member may report misconduct to any Elder. The Elders must investigate reports, including reports about conduct between non-Elders or an incident in which no Elder was present. The detailed investigation, Impeachment Vote, and Sentencing Vote procedure is in Appendix B.

An Elder accused of misconduct may not vote on Administrative Laws or participate in directing the investigation or deciding its outcome during the investigation. They may privately present a defense or evidence to the other Elders. Impeachment and sentencing records remain private except where the chosen sentence requires a public statement, demotion, or banishment.

## 8. Adoption and interpretation

This document is an initial draft for consideration by the current Guild Master and members. It takes effect only when adopted by the Guild through the process the Guild chooses for its initial transition. The one-time transition is intentionally documented separately rather than made a permanent constitutional procedure.

If a matter is not addressed here, the Elders may establish a rule by Administrative Law when it is operational in nature. A rule affecting Guild-wide direction, member rights, election structure, or this Constitution must be brought forward as an Edict. No Administrative Law may amend or override the Bill of Rights.
